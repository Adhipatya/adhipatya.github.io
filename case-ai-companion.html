01 — Overview

AI as a companion for international students

This project explores using artificial intelligence to support international students emotionally and administratively. The goal is bridging support gaps without causing unhealthy user reliance on technology.

02 — Problem

The problem statement

International students frequently face severe loneliness, cultural shock, and overwhelming administrative tasks — often turning to AI for both practical help and emotional support.

68%
68% of students were already using AI for conversational and emotional discussions — a growing, potentially unhealthy reliance on artificial support over human connection.
58%
58% found Swedish administrative processes the most challenging adaptation aspect.
03 — Solution

Two companions, one healthy balance

The solution is a dual-interface application featuring two distinct AI companions. "Alex" handles administrative tasks — like registering for a Personnummer — while "Anna" provides emotional scaffolding and real-world redirection, always pointing users back toward human connection.

The dual-companion concept — Alex for tasks, Anna for reflection
04 — Process

Design process

Research and testing repeatedly surfaced the same ethical tension: an interface warm enough to help, but not so warm it fosters dependence.

Research

Surveyed students and conducted six in-depth interviews to understand where support gaps lived.

Refinement

Shifted to abstract avatars, prioritizing user autonomy and text-based inputs over anthropomorphic cues.

Testing

Evaluated voice-based chibi avatars — but they promoted unwanted emotional attachment, so they were dropped.

Design process — research, refinement, and testing
05 — Ethics

Critical design exploration

To highlight the ethical dangers directly, I also built a deliberate "critical design" version. It used manipulative gamification — like a streak feature that guilt-trips the user — to demonstrate how easily an interface can create unhealthy dependence.

The critical-design variant — showing how streaks and guilt manipulate users
06 — Design

High-fidelity screens

The high-fidelity screens show both companions in action — Alex walking a student through Swedish registration step by step, and Anna offering a private, supportive space for reflection.

Alex & Anna — home and chat experiences
Task guidance and emotional check-in flows
07 — Outcome

Conclusion

Ethical AI must provide support without causing dependence. Using scaffolding and redirection, AI should supplement — not substitute — the human need for real social connection.
